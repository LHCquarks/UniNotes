Take the E-L equations.
$$
\begin{align}
\frac{\partial \mathcal L}{\partial q} &= \frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial \mathcal L}{\partial \dot q}\right)
\end{align}
$$
When $\frac{\partial \mathcal L}{\partial q}$ is $0$, aka $q$ does not appear in the Lagrangian we get that the generalized momentum of the object must be constant, ie:
$$
\begin{align}
0 &= \frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial \mathcal L}{\partial \dot q}\right) \\
P_0 &= \frac{\partial \mathcal L}{\partial \dot q} \\
\end{align}
$$
This concept generalizes into Noether's theorem:

To every continuous symmetry of a system generated my local actions there corresponds a conserved current and vice versa.

This is a very formal version of her theorem but a more "loose" version is

Iff a system has a continuous symmetry then there is a conserved quantity

### WTF is a continuous symmetry
An equilateral triangle has a $60^\circ$ symmetry. This is called a discrete symmetry because the symmetry occurs in fixed increments.

On the other hand a circle has a continuous rotational symmetry because it can be rotated by a continuous variable and still look the same.

A continuous symmetry is where there is a continuous degree of freedom that does not effect the system at all. This occurs when the variable does not appear in the Lagrangian
