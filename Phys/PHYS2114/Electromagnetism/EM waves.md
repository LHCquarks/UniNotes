## From Maxwell's equations
We will use the Lorentz gauge ($\vec \nabla \cdot \vec A + \mu_0\epsilon_0\partial_t\varphi = 0$) for this thus our dynamic Maxwell equations are
$$
\begin{align}
\left(\mu_0\epsilon_0 \partial_t^2 -\nabla ^2 \right)\varphi &= \frac{\rho}{\epsilon_0}\\
\left(\mu_0\epsilon_0\partial^2_t - \nabla^2\right)\vec A &= \mu_0\vec J \\ 
\end{align}
$$
We will also consider empty space so $\rho = 0, \vec J = 0$ and thus:
$$
\begin{align}
\left(\mu_0\epsilon_0 \partial_t^2 -\nabla ^2 \right)\varphi &= 0 \tag{1}\\
\left(\mu_0\epsilon_0\partial^2_t - \nabla^2\right)\vec A &= 0 \tag{2}\\ 
\end{align}
$$
Finally we will also impose the Coulomb gauge because we want to thus
$$
\begin{align}
\vec \nabla \cdot \vec A = 0 \\
\implies 0 + \mu_0\epsilon_0 \partial_t \varphi &= 0 \\
\partial_t \varphi &=0 \\
\varphi &= 0 \\
(1) \implies 0 &= 0 \\
(2) \implies (\mu_0\epsilon_0\partial_t^2 - \nabla^2)\vec A &= 0
\end{align}
$$
