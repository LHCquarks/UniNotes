There exist wave functions in a product space that can not be made from the direct product of two wave functions. An example is sing1:
$$
\begin{align}
\ket {\text{sing}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt 2}\ket {ud} - \ket {du}
\end{align}
$$
In this scenario, one of the particles is in the up state but not both. This means they are opposites.
## Spooky action at a distance
This is fairly counter intuitive and to show this lets make a thought experiment.

Say we have both Alice and Bob, and two electrons. We then entangle the electrons into $\ket {\text{sing}}$ and give Alice one and so to Bob. Then Alice goes to Amsterdam and Bob goes to Berlin.

When Alice then measures the spin of her electron the wave function collapses into either $\ket {ud}$ or $\ket {du}$ in which case Alice knows Bob's electron's wave function before Bob measures it.

This is a little crazy as that information between the two electrons traveled instantaneously (a little faster than the speed of light) a big no no in classical physics. This phenomena was called "spooky action at a distance" by Einstein and was the root of his distaste for QM.

The solution to this conundrum is that to Bob, the process still looks like a random event meaning that Alice is not able to transmit any information to Bob through entanglement and so causality is preserved.
## Entanglement
Entanglement occurs when we have a wave function that can not be separated cleanly into two products ie. $\ket \psi \not = \ket {\psi_A} \otimes \ket {\psi_B}$. When this occurs measurements of one part of the system reveals information about the other part.
